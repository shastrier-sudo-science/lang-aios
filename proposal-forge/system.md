## What This Is

This is not theory. This is the exact system I built inside my own SaaS (ProposalForge) to write client proposals on Fiverr, Upwork, and direct outreach — before I had any clients, any reputation, or any budget.

It works because it treats the proposal as a **diagnostic tool**, not a sales pitch. Most freelancers write proposals that talk about themselves. Winners write proposals that diagnose the client's pain and prescribe the exact fix.

**Use this system when:**
- You are responding to a job post on Fiverr or Upwork
- You are cold-emailing a prospect with a custom proposal
- You are following up on a discovery call with a written scope
- You are pitching a retainer to an existing client

**Do NOT use this system when:**
- The client has not given you enough information to diagnose (ask first)
- You are sending a generic template without customization (this is not a Mad Lib)
- You are desperate and willing to undercut everyone (this system prices on value, not fear)

## The Proposal Anatomy Checklist

Every winning proposal has 7 elements. Miss one, and the proposal weakens. Miss two, and it dies in the client's inbox.

| # | Element | Purpose | What The Client Thinks |
|---|---------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | **The Hook** | Grabs attention in the first 2 sentences | "This person read my brief." |
| 2 | **The Diagnosis** | Restates the client's problem in their own words | "This person understands me." |
| 3 | **The Mechanism** | Explains HOW you will solve it (not just WHAT) | "This person knows their craft." |
| 4 | **The Proof** | One relevant example, stat, or case study | "This person has done this before." |
| 5 | **The Scope** | Exact deliverables, timeline, and boundaries | "I know exactly what I'm buying." |
| 6 | **The Price** | Single number with justification, not a range | "This price makes sense for the value." |
| 7 | **The Next Step** | One specific action to move forward | "I know what to do next." |

**Rule:** If your proposal does not have all 7 elements, rewrite it. No exceptions.

## The Discovery Prompt

Use this prompt in Claude or ChatGPT to extract the client's real needs before you write a single word of the proposal.

```
You are a senior freelance consultant. I have received the following client brief. Your job is to extract the 5 critical pieces of information I need to write a proposal that closes.

CLIENT BRIEF:
[Paste the client's job post, email, or call notes here]

Extract and return ONLY these 5 items:

1. STATED PROBLEM: What does the client SAY they need? (1 sentence)
2. HIDDEN PROBLEM: What do they ACTUALLY need but haven't said? (1 sentence)
3. SUCCESS METRIC: How will they know this project worked? (1 specific number or outcome)
4. BUDGET SIGNAL: What price range are they signaling? (low/medium/high — cite evidence)
5. URGENCY: How fast do they need this? (specific date or "no deadline mentioned")

Do not write the proposal. Do not give advice. Just extract these 5 items in the exact format above.
```

**When to use:** Before every proposal. Even if you think you know what the client wants. The hidden problem is where your price lives.

## The Structure Prompt

Use this prompt to build the proposal skeleton after running the Discovery Prompt.

```
You are a proposal architect. I have completed a client discovery and extracted the following data:

STATED PROBLEM: [paste from Discovery Prompt output]
HIDDEN PROBLEM: [paste from Discovery Prompt output]
SUCCESS METRIC: [paste from Discovery Prompt output]
BUDGET SIGNAL: [paste from Discovery Prompt output]
URGENCY: [paste from Discovery Prompt output]

Write a proposal using the 7-element anatomy. Follow these rules:

1. THE HOOK: Lead with the hidden problem, not the stated problem. (2 sentences max)
2. THE DIAGNOSIS: Restate the client's situation using their own words from the brief. (3-4 sentences)
3. THE MECHANISM: Explain your process in 3 steps. Use active verbs. No jargon. (4-5 sentences)
4. THE PROOF: Cite one relevant result. If you have no client history, use a personal project or a hypothetical with specific numbers. (2 sentences)
5. THE SCOPE: List exact deliverables with deadlines. Use bullet points. (5-7 bullets)
6. THE PRICE: Give a single number for the recommended tier. Justify it in one sentence by linking it to the success metric. (1 sentence)
7. THE NEXT STEP: Give one specific action. "Reply to this email with 'YES' and I'll send the contract." (1 sentence)

Tone: Confident but not arrogant. Expert but not condescending. Like a doctor giving a diagnosis, not a salesperson giving a pitch.

Length: 300-400 words total. No filler. No "I hope this finds you well." No "Please let me know if you have any questions."
```

**When to use:** After Discovery Prompt. Paste the 5 extracted items into the brackets. Run the prompt. Edit the output for your voice.

## The Polish Prompt

Use this prompt to tighten the draft proposal before sending.

```
You are a ruthless editor. I have written a client proposal that needs to be tightened before sending.

MY PROPOSAL DRAFT:
[Paste your proposal here]

Apply these edits in order:

1. REMOVE: Every sentence that talks about YOU ("I have 5 years of experience", "I am passionate about", "I believe"). Replace with sentences that talk about THE CLIENT's outcome.
2. REMOVE: Every hedge word ("just", "maybe", "perhaps", "I think", "hopefully"). Replace with certainty.
3. REMOVE: Every paragraph longer than 3 sentences. Break into bullets or shorter paragraphs.
4. ADD: One specific number or timeframe to the mechanism section. (e.g., "within 48 hours" not "soon")
5. ADD: One risk reversal sentence to the scope section. (e.g., "If the first draft doesn't meet the success metric, I'll revise at no cost.")
6. CHECK: Does the price appear only once? If it appears more than once, remove the extras. The price should be a single confident number, not a negotiation.
7. CHECK: Does the next step require a yes/no response? If not, rewrite it so it does.

Return the polished proposal. Then list the 3 most important changes you made and why.
```

**When to use:** After Structure Prompt. Always run this before hitting send. The difference between a $100 proposal and a $1,000 proposal is usually 3 sentences.

## The Follow-Up Script

Send this exactly 3 days after delivering the proposal. No sooner. No later.

```
Subject: Quick check-in — [Project Name]

Hi [Name],

I sent over the proposal for [project name] on [date]. I know these decisions take time, so I wanted to check in with one quick question:

Is there a specific piece of information I can provide that would make this an easy yes?

No pressure either way. If the timing isn't right, I completely understand. If there's a blocker I can help remove, let me know.

Best,  
[Your Name]
```

**Why this works:** It assumes a delay, not a rejection. It offers help, not pressure. It gives the client an easy out, which paradoxically makes them more likely to say yes.

**If no reply after 7 days:** Send one final message: *"I assume the timing isn't right. I'll close this proposal on [date + 3 days]. If things change, just reply and I'll reopen it."* Then move on. Never chase.

## The Workflow (Your Daily Protocol)

| Step | Action | Time | Tool |
|------|--------|------|------|
| 1 | Paste client brief into Discovery Prompt | 2 min | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 2 | Review 5 extracted items. Identify hidden problem. | 3 min | Your brain |
| 3 | Paste 5 items into Structure Prompt | 2 min | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 4 | Edit draft for your voice and specific client details | 10 min | Your brain |
| 5 | Paste draft into Polish Prompt | 2 min | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 6 | Apply final edits. Send. | 5 min | Email/Fiverr/Upwork |
| 7 | Calendar reminder: follow-up in 3 days | 1 min | Calendar app |

**Total time per proposal: 25 minutes.**

**Rule:** If a proposal takes longer than 30 minutes, you are overthinking it. The client is not reading it that carefully. They are scanning for 3 things: Do you understand me? Can you do it? Is the price fair?
