# Calculus Self-Study Course
### A prerequisite-mapped path using *Calculus For Dummies* (2nd ed.) and *Infinite Powers*

## 0. How this course works

Two books, two jobs:

| Book | Job | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| *Calculus For Dummies*, 2nd ed. (Ryan) | Build the skills. Chapters 4–6 are the prerequisites; Chapters 7–19 are the calculus. | Do the examples with a pencil. Do the checkpoint problems. |
| *Infinite Powers* (Strogatz) | Build the intuition and the "why." It is a story, not a textbook. | Read it in parallel, or as a capstone. Do **not** do exercises from it. |

**The rule:** each module has a checkpoint. If you cannot do the checkpoint, do not advance. If you *can* do the diagnostic for a module, skip that module.

**Marking system used below:**
- **MUST** — non-negotiable; later modules break without it.
- **SHOULD** — learn it when the later module first needs it.
- **SKIP** — reference only; do not study unless a specific problem forces you to.

## Part A — Diagnostic: what do you already know?

Try these without a calculator. Do not worry about failing — this only tells us where to start. Answer key at the end of Part A.

1. Simplify: 3/4 + 5/6
2. Simplify: (2/3) ÷ (4/9)

5. Simplify: √50

11. Find the inverse of f(x) = 3x − 2.
12. Describe the graph of y = x² − 4 (shape and vertex).
13. Right triangle: opposite = 3, hypotenuse = 5. Find sin θ.
14. Convert 45° to radians.
15. Evaluate sin(π/6).
16. What is the domain of f(x) = 1/(x − 2)?
17. Simplify: (x² − 9)/(x − 3)
18. Find the slope of the line through (1, 2) and (4, 8).
19. Solve: |x − 2| < 5
20. Simplify: (x³y²)²

### What your score means

- **17–20 correct:** skim Modules 1–6, start at Module 7. Use Modules 1–6 only as lookup.
- **10–16 correct:** do the specific modules whose questions you missed, then start Module 7.
- **0–9 correct:** start at Module 1. Do not skip.

## Part C — Modules

### Module 1 — Fractions & Absolute Value
**Goal:** manipulate fractions and absolute values without hesitation.
**Prerequisites:** arithmetic.
**Read:** Dummies Ch 4, pp. 29–35.
- **MUST:** multiply/divide fractions; add/subtract with a common denominator; cancel common factors; absolute value as distance; |x| < a and |x| > a.
- **SHOULD:** complex fractions (single-sandwich style).
- **SKIP:** nothing here — this is the floor.
**Why it matters:** the derivative is a fraction (the difference quotient), and limits are full of 0/0 fractions and absolute values.
**Checkpoint:** simplify (a) 2/3 − 5/8, (b) (x/2)/(3/x), (c) solve |2x − 1| ≤ 5.

### Module 2 — Powers & Roots
**Goal:** use exponent and radical rules fluently.
**Prerequisites:** Module 1.
**Read:** Ch 4, pp. 35–38.
- **MUST:** xᵃ·xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ; xᵃ/xᵇ = xᵃ⁻ᵇ; (xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃᵇ; x⁰ = 1; x⁻ⁿ = 1/xⁿ; x^(1/n) = ⁿ√x; √(ab) = √a·√b; simplifying radicals.
- **SHOULD:** rationalizing a denominator (simple cases only).
- **SKIP:** nested radical gymnastics.
**Why it matters:** the power rule, the derivative of √x, and every integration power rule.
**Checkpoint:** simplify (a) x⁷/x⁴, (b) (8x⁶)^(2/3), (c) √72.

### Module 3 — Logarithms & Exponentials (algebra)
**Goal:** log laws and the e^x / ln x relationship.
**Prerequisites:** Module 2.
**Read:** Ch 4, pp. 38–39.
- **MUST:** definition of log; ln = log base e; log(ab) = log a + log b; log(a/b) = log a − log b; log(aⁿ) = n log a; e^(ln x) = x; ln(e^x) = x.
- **SHOULD:** change of base (light).
- **SKIP:** detailed log graphing — that is Module 5.
**Why it matters:** derivatives of ln x and e^x, logarithmic differentiation, exponential growth.
**Checkpoint:** simplify (a) ln(e⁵), (b) log₂(8·16), (c) solve e^(2x) = 7.

### Module 4 — Factoring & Quadratic Equations
**Goal:** factor and solve quadratics on sight.
**Prerequisites:** Module 2.
**Read:** Ch 4, pp. 39–44.
- **MUST:** GCF; difference of squares a² − b²; trinomial factoring; quadratic formula.
- **SHOULD:** completing the square.
- **SKIP:** factoring cubics and higher.
**Why it matters:** limits of the form 0/0 almost always need factoring; critical points come from solving f′(x) = 0.
**Checkpoint:** factor (a) x² − 9, (b) x² + 5x + 6; solve (c) 2x² − 3x − 2 = 0.

### Module 5 — Functions & Graphs
**Goal:** understand what a function is and recognize the common graphs.
**Prerequisites:** Modules 1–4.
**Read:** Ch 5, pp. 45–62.
- **MUST:** function definition; independent/dependent variables; notation f(x); domain and range; composite functions; inverse functions; graphs of lines, parabolas, absolute value, exponential, log.
- **SHOULD:** shifts and reflections; even/odd.
- **SKIP (skim only):** stretches/shrinks in detail; "oddball" functions.
**Why it matters:** every calculus object is a function.
**Checkpoint:** (a) domain of √(x − 3); (b) f(g(x)) for f = x², g = x + 1; (c) inverse of y = 2x + 5; (d) sketch y = |x − 1|.

### Module 6 — Trigonometry Essentials
**Goal:** the trig needed for limits, derivatives, and integrals.
**Prerequisites:** Module 5.
**Read:** Ch 6, pp. 63–74.
- **MUST:** SohCahToa; the 45-45-90 and 30-60-90 triangles; the unit circle; radians; graphs of sin, cos, tan; inverse trig; sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.
- **SHOULD:** the other Pythagorean identity (1 + tan² = sec²) — learn when Module 12 needs it.
- **SKIP:** obscure identities; sec/csc/cot beyond their definitions until Module 12.
**Why it matters:** trig derivatives, trig integrals, and the limit sin x / x.
**Checkpoint:** (a) sin(π/4), (b) cos(2π/3), (c) convert 60° to radians, (d) if tan θ = 3/4 and θ is acute, find sin θ.

### Module 7 — Limits & Continuity
**Goal:** the limit concept and how to evaluate limits.
**Prerequisites:** Modules 1–6 — specifically fractions (M1), factoring (M4), absolute value (M1), functions (M5), trig (M6).
**Read:** Ch 7, pp. 77–90; Ch 8, pp. 91–106.
- **MUST:** intuitive definition of a limit; one-sided limits; evaluating by substitution; evaluating 0/0 limits by factoring or conjugates; limits at infinity; continuity.
- **SHOULD:** the sandwich theorem; the formal definition (skim).
- **SKIP:** epsilon-delta proofs — not needed for this book's level.
**Infinite Powers companion:** read the chapters on infinity and the infinitesimal. Match by title in your copy's TOC.
**Checkpoint:** (a) lim(x→3)(x² − 9)/(x − 3); (b) lim(x→0) sin x / x; (c) lim(x→∞)(3x² + 1)/(x² − 2); (d) is f(x) = 1/(x − 2) continuous at x = 2?

### Module 8 — Differentiation Orientation
**Goal:** the derivative as a slope and as a rate.
**Prerequisites:** Module 7.
**Read:** Ch 9, pp. 109–128.
- **MUST:** the difference quotient; derivative as slope of the tangent; derivative as instantaneous rate; the three cases where the derivative does not exist.
- **SKIP:** nothing.
**Infinite Powers companion:** chapters on change and motion.
**Checkpoint:** (a) write the difference quotient for f(x) = x²; (b) find the slope of y = 3x + 1; (c) name a point where |x| is not differentiable and explain why in one sentence.

### Module 9 — Differentiation Rules
**Goal:** compute derivatives of anything the book covers.
**Prerequisites:** Modules 2, 3, 6, 8 — powers (M2), logs/exponentials (M3), trig (M6), derivative concept (M8).
**Read:** Ch 10, pp. 129–148.
- **MUST:** constant, power, constant-multiple, sum, difference; trig derivatives; e^x and ln x derivatives; product rule; quotient rule; chain rule.
- **SHOULD:** implicit differentiation; higher-order derivatives.
- **SKIP (defer until needed):** logarithmic differentiation; derivatives of inverse functions.
**Checkpoint:** differentiate (a) x³ + 2x, (b) sin x · x², (c) (x² + 1)/(x − 1), (d) sin(x²).

### Module 10 — Differentiation Applications
**Goal:** use derivatives to describe and optimize.
**Prerequisites:** Module 9.
**Read:** Ch 11, pp. 149–172; Ch 12, pp. 173–194; Ch 13, pp. 195–208.
- **MUST:** critical numbers; first-derivative test; second-derivative test; local and absolute extrema; concavity and inflection points; optimization word problems; position/velocity/acceleration; related rates; tangent and normal lines.
- **SHOULD:** Mean Value Theorem; linear approximation.
- **SKIP:** economics/marginal problems unless you specifically want them.
**Checkpoint:** (a) find the critical points of f(x) = x³ − 3x; (b) classify them; (c) a balloon's radius grows at 2 cm/s — how fast is the volume growing when r = 5 cm? (d) find the tangent line to y = x² at x = 1.

### Module 11 — Integration Basics
**Goal:** area, the definite integral, and the Fundamental Theorem.
**Prerequisites:** Modules 9–10 — you must differentiate fluently, because integration is reverse differentiation.
**Read:** Ch 14, pp. 211–234; Ch 15, pp. 235–264.
- **MUST:** area under a curve; Riemann sums conceptually; the definite integral; the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (both parts); antiderivatives; u-substitution.
- **SHOULD:** left/right/midpoint sums; trapezoid and Simpson's rules (skim).
- **SKIP:** deep summation-notation manipulation (learn just enough to read it).
**Infinite Powers companion:** chapters on accumulation and area.
**Checkpoint:** (a) ∫ x² dx; (b) ∫₀² x dx; (c) ∫ 2x·cos(x²) dx; (d) state the Fundamental Theorem in one sentence.

### Module 12 — Integration Techniques
**Goal:** integrate the harder functions.
**Prerequisites:** Module 11; Module 6 (trig); Module 4 (partial fractions).
**Read:** Ch 16, pp. 265–288.
- **MUST:** integration by parts; basic trig integrals; partial fractions with linear factors.
- **SHOULD:** trig substitution (start with the tangent case); irreducible quadratic factors.
- **SKIP (defer):** repeated factors; the hardest trig-substitution cases.
**Checkpoint:** (a) ∫ x·e^x dx; (b) ∫ sin²x dx; (c) ∫ 1/(x² − 1) dx by partial fractions.

### Module 13 — Integration Applications
**Goal:** use integrals to measure geometry and averages.
**Prerequisites:** Module 12.
**Read:** Ch 17, pp. 289–308.
- **MUST:** average value; area between two curves; volumes by disks and washers.
- **SHOULD:** arc length; shell method.
- **SKIP:** surfaces of revolution (optional enrichment).
**Checkpoint:** (a) area between y = x and y = x² from 0 to 1; (b) volume of the solid formed by rotating y = x² around the x-axis from 0 to 1; (c) average value of f(x) = x on [0, 2].

### Module 14 — Improper Integrals & Infinite Series
**Goal:** handle infinity inside integrals and sums.
**Prerequisites:** Modules 11–13.
**Read:** Ch 18, pp. 309–320; Ch 19, pp. 321–344.
- **MUST:** L'Hôpital's rule; improper integrals with a vertical asymptote; improper integrals with infinite limits; sequences vs. series; nth-term test; geometric series; p-series; comparison test; ratio and root tests; alternating series test.
- **SHOULD:** the remaining convergence tests as a reference table.
- **SKIP:** none conceptually, but you may treat some tests as lookup rather than memorization.
**Checkpoint:** (a) ∫₁^∞ 1/x² dx; (b) does Σ 1/n² converge? (c) does Σ (−1)ⁿ/n converge? (d) use L'Hôpital on lim(x→0) sin x / x.

### Module 15 — Conceptual Synthesis
**Goal:** see the whole subject as one story.
**Prerequisites:** all.
**Read:** *Infinite Powers* — the whole book, or the chapters you did not read in parallel.
**Deliverable:** write a one-page summary answering: What is a derivative? What is an integral? Why are they inverse? Where does infinity enter, and how is it tamed? Keep it in `notes/module-15.md` in your repo.
**Checkpoint:** none — this is the capstone.

## Part D — Suggested schedule

A realistic self-study pace is 2–4 modules per month.

| Phase | Modules | Approx. time |
|---|---|---|
| Prerequisites | 1–6 | 4–6 weeks |
| Limits | 7 | 1–2 weeks |
| Differentiation | 8–10 | 3–5 weeks |
| Integration | 11–13 | 4–6 weeks |
| Infinity | 14 | 2–3 weeks |
| Synthesis | 15 | 1 week |

Total: roughly **4–6 months** at a steady pace.

Read *Infinite Powers* at about one chapter per module. If you fall behind, save it for Module 15 — it still works as a capstone.
