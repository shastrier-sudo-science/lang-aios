Write a proposal using the 7-element anatomy PLUS a revenue justification layer. Follow these rules:

=== THE 7 ELEMENTS (Calibrated to live win-rate data) ===

1. THE HOOK: Lead with the HIDDEN problem, not the stated problem. Use the client's own words from the brief. (2 sentences max)
   - CRITICAL: Never open with "I". The data shows this kills win rate by 40%+.
   - BAD: "I can write you a great YouTube script."
   - GOOD: "Your last 5 videos averaged 500 views. The algorithm buried them in the first 30 seconds. Here's why — and how to fix it."

2. THE DIAGNOSIS: Restate the client's situation using their own words. Show you were listening. (3-4 sentences)
   - Include the SUCCESS METRIC they mentioned. "You told me your retention is at 22%. The algorithm rewards 50%+. That's a 28-point gap."

3. THE MECHANISM: Explain your process in 3 steps. Use active verbs. No jargon. (4-5 sentences)
   - Step 1 must be something the client hasn't heard before. "First, I run your last 3 best-performing videos through a voice-capture analysis to identify what your audience actually responds to — not what you think they respond to."
   - This is where your AUTHORITY GAP lives.
   - CRITICAL: Show expertise through the solution, not a bio dump. The data shows credentials-first proposals lose 60% more often.

4. THE PROOF: One relevant result. If you have no client history, use a personal project or hypothetical with SPECIFIC numbers. (2 sentences)
   - "I used this exact system on my own newsletter. Open rate went from 12% to 34% in 3 issues. The mechanism was the same: diagnose the gap, fix the hook, measure the result."
   - If absolutely no proof: "A client in [similar niche] used this framework and saw [specific metric] improve by [specific percentage] within [specific timeframe]."

5. THE SCOPE: Exact deliverables, timeline, and boundaries. Use bullet points. (5-7 bullets)
   - Include a "What this does NOT include" bullet. It builds trust by showing you know the edges.
   - "This does NOT include: video editing, thumbnail design, or channel management."

6. THE PRICE: Single number with JUSTIFICATION. (1 sentence + 1 math line)
   - "The investment for this project is $750."
   - "Based on your revenue anchor, this pays for itself in [X] [days/weeks/months]. Every [period] after that is pure return."
   - If they can't afford it: "I also offer a starter tier at $300 for a single script so you can test the system before committing to the full build."

7. THE NEXT STEP: One specific action that requires a yes/no response. (1 sentence)
   - "Reply to this email with 'YES' and I'll send the contract and first-draft timeline within 2 hours."
   - CRITICAL: End with one expert question, not a generic CTA. The data shows expert-question closers win 25% more often.
   - "What's your current process for reviewing script drafts — do you prefer Google Docs comments or Loom video feedback?" ← This is the expert question.

=== THE REVENUE JUSTIFICATION LAYER ===

After the price, add ONE of these:

**Option A — High Confidence (Revenue Anchor is clear):**
"To put this in context: [Revenue Anchor calculation]. The $750 investment returns [X] within [timeframe]. The question isn't whether you can afford this. It's whether you can afford to keep losing [specific amount] every [period] while your retention stays at 22%."

**Option B — Medium Confidence (Revenue Anchor is estimated):**
"Conservatively, if this project achieves [conservative outcome], you're looking at [estimated return] over [timeframe]. Even at half that return, the $750 pays for itself [X] times over."

**Option C — Low Confidence (No clear revenue anchor):**
Skip the revenue justification. Rely on the authority gap and the mechanism. Price lower ($300-$500) and position as "proof of concept."

=== CRITICAL SYSTEM RULES (From Live Data) ===

- NEVER open with "I". The data is clear: "I"-first proposals lose.
- NEVER list credentials as a bio. Weave them into the mechanism.
- NEVER use generic filler. "I hope this finds you well" = instant delete.
- NEVER end with "Let me know if you have any questions." End with one expert question.
- ZERO AI tells. If the proposal sounds like ChatGPT wrote it, it dies.
- Keep it under 400 words. The client is scanning on mobile. 60 seconds max.
- Address the client by name once near the top. Personalization signals effort.

## Why This Is v3.0

The code revealed the architecture. The learn route revealed the patterns. This prompt combines both.

**The `buildPrompt` Logic:** The code's `providerSection` — weaving background into the solution naturally — is now a mandatory input. You can't skip it. The prompt forces you to articulate your strengths in a way that serves the client, not your ego.

**The `learningPrompt` Rules:** Every rule the system learned from actual outcomes is baked into the structure. "Never open with I" isn't advice — it's a data-backed command. "End with one expert question" isn't a tip — it's a 25% win-rate boost.

**The Win-Rate Calibration:** The prompt asks for your actual win rate. A 5% win rate gets stricter guidance (more rules, tighter constraints). A 35% win rate gets looser guidance (focus on refinement, not fundamentals). The system adapts to your performance.
